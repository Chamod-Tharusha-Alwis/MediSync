About this disease: Dengue

What it is

  • A viral infection caused by the dengue virus, carried mainly by Aedes aegypti mosquitoes.

How it spreads

  • Spread by the bite of an infected female mosquito; mosquitoes pick up the virus by biting infected people, including people without symptoms.
  • An infected mosquito can pass on the virus for the rest of its life.

Common symptoms

  • Many people have mild or no symptoms and recover in 1 to 2 weeks.
  • Symptoms usually start 4 to 10 days after infection and last 2 to 7 days: high fever, severe headache, pain behind the eyes, muscle and joint pain, nausea, vomiting, swollen glands and rash.

Danger signs and when to seek urgent care

  • Severe symptoms often come after the fever goes down: severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, rapid breathing, bleeding gums or nose, blood in vomit or stool, restlessness, extreme thirst, pale or cold skin, weakness.
  • Anyone with these signs should get medical care immediately.

Prevention and what the public can do

  • Avoid bites, especially in the daytime: clothing that covers the body, mosquito nets, window screens and repellents containing DEET, picaridin or IR3535.
  • Stop mosquito breeding: remove containers that hold standing water, dispose of waste properly, and cover, empty and clean water storage every week.

Note for clinicians (testing and care)

  • There is no specific treatment; care is supportive (rest, fluids, paracetamol). NSAIDs such as ibuprofen and aspirin are avoided because they can increase bleeding risk.
  • Laboratory confirmation uses nucleic acid amplification tests, ELISA or rapid tests; direct detection methods are most useful in the first week of illness.
